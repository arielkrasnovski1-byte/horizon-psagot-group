Glossary

Glossary — stocks and crypto

58 terms you meet in the investing world — explained briefly, in plain language.

Basics

Asset
Anything of value that can be held or traded — a stock, a bond, a digital coin, real estate.
Capital gain
The profit made when an asset is sold for more than it cost. Usually taxable.
Commission / fee
A payment to whoever executes a transaction or holds assets for you — buying, selling, converting or managing.
Compound interest
Returns earned on previous returns as well, so the amount grows at an accelerating pace over time.
Exchange rate
The price of one currency in terms of another. It affects the value of foreign-currency investments.
Inflation
A general rise in prices that erodes the purchasing power of money over time.
Interest rate
The price of money: what you pay on a loan or earn on a deposit. The central-bank rate affects every market.
Liquidity
How quickly and easily an asset can be turned into cash without materially moving its price.
Management fee
An annual charge, as a percentage of the amount managed, paid to whoever runs a fund or portfolio.
Return
The gain or loss on an investment, usually expressed as a percentage of the amount invested.
Time horizon
The length of time you plan to hold an investment before you need the money.
Trading account
An account at a bank or broker through which securities are bought and sold.

Stocks & markets

Bear market
A prolonged period of falling prices and market pessimism.
Bond
A loan from the investor to a government or company, in return for interest and repayment of principal at a set date.
Bull market
A prolonged period of rising prices and market optimism.
Dividend
A portion of a company’s profits distributed to shareholders. It is not guaranteed.
Dividend yield
The annual dividend divided by the share price, as a percentage.
ETF
A fund that trades on an exchange like a stock and tracks an index or a basket of assets.
Index
A theoretical basket of securities reflecting the performance of a market or sector, such as an index of a country’s largest companies.
IPO
The first time a company offers its shares to the public on an exchange.
Market / limit order
A market order executes immediately at the available price; a limit order executes only at your set price or better.
Market capitalization
The total market value of a company: share price multiplied by the number of shares.
Mutual fund
A fund that pools money from many investors and invests it according to a defined policy.
P/E ratio
Share price divided by earnings per share. A common measure for comparing how companies are priced.
Quarterly report
A report a public company publishes every quarter on its revenue, profit and condition.
Spread
The gap between an asset’s buy and sell price at a given moment — a hidden cost of trading.
Stock / share
A share of ownership in a company. The holder participates in both its rise and fall in value.
Stock exchange
A regulated marketplace where securities are traded under set rules.

Crypto

Altcoin
A name for any cryptocurrency other than Bitcoin.
Bitcoin
The first cryptocurrency, with a limited supply fixed in advance in its code.
Blockchain
A shared public record of transactions, organized as a chain of blocks that is very hard to alter after the fact.
Cryptocurrency
A digital asset that runs on a decentralized network and is secured by cryptography.
DeFi
Financial services that run on a blockchain through smart contracts, without an intermediary.
Ethereum
A blockchain network that can run smart contracts and applications; its coin is called Ether.
Exchange
A platform where cryptocurrencies are bought, sold and converted.
Gas fee
The payment to the network for processing an action, which varies with congestion.
Halving
A scheduled event in which the Bitcoin mining reward is cut in half, roughly every four years.
Hot / cold wallet
A hot wallet is online and convenient; a cold wallet is offline and safer for storage.
Mining
The process by which computers validate transactions and add blocks to the network in exchange for a reward.
Phishing
A scam that impersonates a trusted party to steal passwords or a seed phrase.
Private key
A secret code that allows assets to be moved. Whoever holds it controls the asset.
Seed phrase
A sequence of words that restores the entire wallet. It must never be shared with anyone.
Smart contract
A program running on a blockchain that executes automatically when predefined conditions are met.
Stablecoin
A digital coin designed to hold a steady value, usually pegged to the dollar. It carries risks too.
Staking
Locking up coins to help secure the network in exchange for a reward. It carries its own risks.
Wallet
A tool that manages the digital keys giving access to your crypto assets.

Risk & portfolio

Asset allocation
How a portfolio is divided between asset types — stocks, bonds, cash, crypto.
Correction
A significant price drop after a period of gains, not necessarily the start of a crisis.
Diversification
Spreading investments across different assets so that damage to one does not sink the whole portfolio.
Dollar-cost averaging
Investing a fixed amount at regular intervals, without trying to time the market.
FOMO
Fear of missing out: the urge to buy simply because the price is rising and everyone is talking about it.
Leverage
Investing with borrowed money. It magnifies potential gains — and losses just as much.
Ponzi scheme
A fraud in which “returns” to earlier investors are paid from new investors’ money, until it collapses.
Portfolio
The full set of assets an investor holds.
Rebalancing
Bringing a portfolio back to its original mix after price moves have shifted it.
Risk
The possibility that the outcome differs from what was expected — including losing some or all of the investment.
Sentiment
The overall mood of investors — optimism or fear — which influences prices.
Volatility
How strongly an asset’s price moves. High volatility means sharp rises and falls.
