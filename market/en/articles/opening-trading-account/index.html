How to Open a Trading Account — Bank vs. Investment House

A beginner's guide to what a trading account is, how trading through a bank differs from using an investment house or broker, and what to compare before choosing.

What Is a Trading Account?

To buy and sell securities — shares, bonds, exchange-traded funds and more — you cannot go to the exchange directly. You need an authorised intermediary that passes on your buy and sell orders and holds the securities for you. A trading account is the account you open with that intermediary: your money is deposited in it, your orders go through it, and the securities you own are recorded in it.

In Israel there are two main routes: the bank where you keep your current account, or an investment house or broker — firms that specialise in securities trading. Both lead to the same markets, but they differ in cost, service and day-to-day experience.

Trading Through a Bank

The bank's main advantage is convenience. The account already exists, the money is there, and everything sits in one place with your salary, savings and loans. Usually no separate sign-up is needed either.

On the other hand, bank fees tend to be higher. It is common to pay a commission on every purchase and sale, and often a custody fee too — a periodic charge simply for holding securities in the account. Some fees are negotiable, but customers who do not ask usually pay the full list price.

Trading Through an Investment House or Broker

Investment houses and brokers specialise in trading, so they generally offer lower commissions, more advanced trading platforms and sometimes access to a wider range of global markets. Many charge no custody fee, but there may be a monthly account fee or a minimum commission, mainly on accounts with little activity.

The trade-off is a little more effort: you open a separate account, transfer money to it from your bank and keep track of two places instead of one. There are also foreign brokers operating outside Israel. Anyone choosing that route should know that reporting and paying tax may be their own responsibility, whereas Israeli institutions usually withhold tax at source.

What Does Opening an Account Involve?

The process is now mostly online and usually includes a few standard steps:

  • Identification — a photo of your ID, and sometimes a video call.
  • Details of the bank account the money will come from and return to.
  • A questionnaire about your knowledge, experience, financial situation and source of funds.
  • Tax declarations, for example about residency or citizenship in another country.
  • Signing an agreement and a risk disclosure document, and — once approved — a first deposit.

Some questions may feel intrusive, but they stem from legal rules on money laundering and customer protection.

What to Compare Before You Choose

  • Buy and sell commissions — the rate, and the minimum charge per trade.
  • Custody and account fees — fixed charges you pay even when you make no trades.
  • Currency conversion — the cost of converting shekels into foreign currency, relevant to anyone trading abroad.
  • Minimum amount — whether an initial deposit is required, and how large.
  • Service and support — in which language, hours and channels you can get help.
  • Licensing and regulation — which authority supervises the firm, and in which country.
No single trading account is "the best" for everyone — the real cost depends on the amount, how often you trade and the markets you trade in.

So look at the whole picture, not one figure. A low commission per trade is not necessarily worthwhile if it comes with a monthly account fee, and vice versa.

Risks and Things to Keep in Mind

Opening a trading account is only a technical step, but what follows carries real risk. The value of securities rises and falls, and you can lose some or even all of your money. Small-looking fees add up over time and erode the result.

Make sure the firm you choose is properly licensed and supervised, understand the tax implications in advance, and be wary of unsolicited approaches promising quick profits. Above all, do not rush: learn first, and start only once you understand what is involved.
