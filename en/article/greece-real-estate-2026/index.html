Real Estate Investments in Greece: The 2026 Complete Guide

Real Estate Abroad 02.04.2026 7 min read By: Horizon Psagot Group

Why Greece Attracts Israeli Investors

In recent years Greece has become a sought-after destination for Israeli investors looking beyond the local market. Geographic proximity, a short flight, a familiar Mediterranean climate and entry prices that are relatively low compared with Israel combine into an attractive proposition. Cities such as Thessaloniki and Athens offer a lively urban fabric, rental demand from students, professionals and tourists, and potential for value appreciation. For an investor who finds the domestic market expensive and saturated, Greece offers a more accessible starting point.

Attractiveness, however, is no guarantee of success. Investing abroad requires the same disciplined due diligence as investing at home, and sometimes more, given the distance and the different regulatory environment.

What to Examine Before Buying

Due diligence begins with location. A central neighborhood with steady rental demand is fundamentally different from a cheap peripheral area where tenants are hard to find. Understanding the property's target audience matters: a student apartment near a university behaves very differently from a holiday flat intended for short-term rental.

  • Real net yield after management fees, taxes and vacancy periods.
  • The legal and planning status of the property, including clean title and a check for historic debts.
  • Local tax implications at purchase, during holding, on rental income and on future sale.
  • Renovation and maintenance costs, especially in the older buildings common in city centers.
  • A reliable remote-management mechanism, since the investor is not on the ground.

Remote Management and Legal Aspects

One of the central challenges of foreign investment is the gap between the concept and day-to-day execution. Collecting rent, handling repairs, paying taxes and communicating with tenants all require a local presence or a professional management company. It is advisable to engage an independent local lawyer, separate from the seller or the agent, to review the contract and the registration of rights. Integrated guidance that combines a financial, legal and tax perspective significantly reduces the risk of costly mistakes.

A sound investment in Greece is measured not by the low entry price, but by the real yield that remains in your pocket after all expenses and risks.

The Risks to Weigh

Alongside the opportunity there are risks. The Greek property market has experienced historic volatility, and macroeconomic conditions can affect both asset values and rental demand. Older buildings may hide unexpected renovation costs. Regulatory changes, particularly around short-term rentals, can alter the economics of an investment. Poor remote management can erode returns even on a good property.

The conclusion is not to avoid investing, but to approach it with open eyes. Professional due diligence, integrated guidance and a realistic understanding of yield and risk are what separate an informed investment from an adventure. Every investor's circumstances differ, so it is advisable to tailor each decision to your personal profile and to seek individual advice.
